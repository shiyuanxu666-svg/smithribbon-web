Ribbon Crock / Rub-Fastness for Printed Logo & Brand-Mark Programs — Mill-Side Spec, ISFA 116 / ISO 105-X12 / AATCC 8 Dry-Wet-Perspiration Fastness

If your ribbon program touches any printed logo, brand-mark, monogram, retail-store-name, co-branded merchandise-mark, hot-stamped foil, woven-jacquard label, or screen-printed graphic — gift packaging, beauty & fragrance wrap, jewelry boxes, apparel hangtag-ribbon, holiday co-branded merchandise, wedding-favor ribbon, corporate-event ribbon, or any end-use where the ribbon functions as a brand-identity surface — the crock / rub-fastness specification is the difference between a brand-quality halo and a "the print rubbed off" complaint. Most brand-buyer spec sheets stop at Delta-E color match (covered in module 148) and OEKO-TEX safety (covered in earlier modules), leaving crock to the mill's "standard" — which for most mid-tier Chinese mills means dry crock grade 3-4 (visible stain after 10 rubs) and wet crock grade 2-3 (significant transfer on contact with moisture).

This module — #152 in the Smith Ribbon brand-buyer mill-side architecture series — gives you the crock / rub-fastness playbook: how to specify crock using ISFA 116 (the narrow-fabric industry standard), ISO 105-X12 (the global textile standard), and AATCC 8 (the North American standard); how to grade dry crock vs wet crock vs perspiration fastness vs sublimation / bleed-resistance; how to select ink-chemistry (water-based, plastisol, dye-sublimation, pigment, foil-stamp, woven-jacquard); and how to write a printed-ribbon spec that protects brand-mark clarity across the supply-chain lifecycle.

1. Why Crock Fastness Is a Different Problem from Color Match & Lightfastness

Color match (Delta-E, module 148) measures whether the printed logo color matches your brand-standard Pantone reference at delivery. Lightfastness (module 151) measures whether the color survives UV / ozone exposure over time. Crock fastness measures a third, independent dimension: whether the printed ink transfers from the printed-face to another surface (white-ground crock-test fabric, an adjacent ribbon layer, the consumer's hand, the inner-pack) under mechanical rubbing and contact with moisture.

The three specs are independent — a ribbon can pass Delta-E ≤ 1.00 at delivery, ISO 105-B02 grade 6 lightfastness, AND fail crock at grade 2 (heavy transfer) within 30 days of stacked storage. The most common "the print looks smudged" complaint is not a Delta-E failure or a lightfastness failure — it is a crock failure caused by ink-chemistry mismatch with substrate, insufficient cure, or missing release-paper interleave between spool layers.

Three factors make ribbon crock uniquely challenging:

  • Ink-substrate compatibility: Water-based ink behaves differently on polyester vs cotton vs nylon vs velvet vs organza vs foil-stamp. The cure-temperature, cure-time, and ink-additive-recipe must be calibrated to substrate — generic "print on ribbon" misses this entirely.
  • Narrow-fabric geometry: Ribbon's narrow width (3mm to 50mm typical) and high length-to-width ratio (1:100 to 1:1000) creates a high contact-area-to-mass ratio in stacked-spool storage. Ink migration is faster on narrow-fabric than on wide-fabric because the contact-pressure per unit-area is higher.
  • Two-sided printing asymmetry: Printed ribbon is typically single-face printed. The back side is unprinted and often undyed. Crock transfer from printed-face to unprinted-face (fabric-to-fabric crock) is the most common failure mode for stacked-spool programs.

2. Crock Test Methods — ISFA 116 / ISO 105-X12 / AATCC 8

Three test methods are commonly referenced in brand-buyer printed-ribbon specs. They are functionally similar but have technical differences:

ISFA 116 is the narrow-fabric industry-standard reference developed by the International Special Fabrics Association specifically for ribbon / tape / webbing. ISO 105-X12 is the global textile reference. AATCC 8 is the parallel North American textile reference. All three use the same crockmeter geometry (100 mm stroke, 900 g weight) and the same gray-scale grading (1 = heavy transfer, 5 = no transfer). For brand programs, specifying all three is acceptable; specifying just ISFA 116 is the cleanest for narrow-fabric programs.

2.1 Dry Crock Test Procedure

The dry crock test uses a dry white-ground crock-test fabric (undyed cotton or undyed polyester per the test method), mounted on the crockmeter finger. The crockmeter finger is rubbed across the printed ribbon surface for 10 strokes (back-and-forth, 100 mm stroke length). The white-ground fabric is then graded against the AATCC / ISO gray-scale for staining. Grade 5 = no visible stain; grade 4 = slight stain visible only at certain angles; grade 3 = visible stain; grade 2 = significant stain; grade 1 = heavy stain. Most brand programs specify dry crock grade 4-5 minimum.

2.2 Wet Crock Test Procedure

The wet crock test is identical to the dry crock test except the white-ground crock-test fabric is wetted with distilled water (or simulated-perspiration solution per ISO 105-E04). Wet crock is more demanding than dry crock because the water / perspiration swells the ink-film and increases transfer-rate. Wet crock grade 3-4 is the typical brand-buyer spec; grade 4-5 is the premium spec. Below grade 3, the ribbon will visibly transfer ink on contact with moisture (rain, hand-cream, sweat, condensation).

2.3 Perspiration Fastness Test (ISO 105-E04)

Perspiration fastness is a separate test method that exposes the printed ribbon to a synthetic-perspiration solution (pH 5.5 acid + pH 8.0 alkaline) at 37 degrees C for 4 hours. The ribbon is then graded for color-change AND for staining on adjacent white-ground fabric. Perspiration fastness is relevant for ribbon worn as an accessory (hair-bow, wrist-bow, hat-trim, scarf-trim, boutonniere). Specify ISO 105-E04 grade 3-4 for accessory-end-use ribbon; downgraded to optional for pure gift-packaging ribbon.

2.4 Sublimation / Bleed-Resistance Test

Sublimation / bleed-resistance is a stability test for printed-ribbon under heat and humidity. The printed ribbon is exposed to 180-200 degrees C x 30 seconds (heat-pressing condition) or 40 degrees C x 90% RH x 7 days (warehouse-storage condition). The ribbon is then inspected for: (a) ink-bleed (ink spreading beyond the print-edge); (b) ink-fade (ink color-shift); (c) ink-transfer to adjacent surface. For dye-sublimation-printed ribbon, this is the critical test; sublimation dyes are inherently heat-sensitive. Specify sublimation-grade dye-chemistry for heat-exposed end-uses (heat-press finishing, hot-climate shipping).

4. Fabric-to-Fabric Crock — The Stacked-Spool Ghost-Image Problem

Fabric-to-fabric crock is the crock transfer that occurs when printed ribbon is wound on a spool and the printed-face contacts the unprinted-face of the adjacent ribbon layer. Over storage time (30 to 90 days at ambient warehouse temperature, accelerated at 40 degrees C / 60% RH), the ink transfers from the printed-face to the unprinted-face, creating a visible ghost-image on the previously-clean ribbon. This is the single most common quality complaint for stacked printed-ribbon programs, especially for:

  • Long-spool programs (100m, 200m, 500m spools) where the inner-layers are under higher contact-pressure
  • High-temperature-shipment programs (container temperatures can reach 50-60 degrees C in summer)
  • Long-warehouse-storage programs (60-90 days between manufacture and retail-shelf)
  • Water-based ink programs where the ink-film has not fully cross-linked

Test method is internal: wind 50 meters of printed ribbon on a standard spool, store 30 days at 40 degrees C / 60% RH, then unwind and inspect for ghost-image on unprinted-face. Accept spec is no visible ghost-image at 30-day inspection. Two mitigation strategies: (1) specify a release-paper or tissue-paper interleave between ribbon layers (adds 0.5-2% cost but eliminates the failure mode); (2) specify a higher-cure cross-linked ink recipe that reduces migration (adds 5-10% cost, no interleave needed).

6. Writing a Crock Spec That Protects Your Printed-Ribbon Program

Combine five parameters into a single printed-ribbon crock specification:

  1. Test method: Specify ISFA 116 (preferred for narrow-fabric) or ISO 105-X12 / AATCC 8 for global / NA programs.
  2. Dry crock grade: Specify grade 4-5 minimum for standard programs, grade 5 for premium luxury programs.
  3. Wet crock grade: Specify grade 3-4 minimum for gift-packaging, grade 4-5 for accessory or premium programs.
  4. Perspiration fastness: Specify ISO 105-E04 (pH 5.5 + pH 8.0, 4 hours x 37 degrees C), grade 3-4 for accessory-end-use ribbon.
  5. Fabric-to-fabric crock + release-paper interleave: Specify the 30-day x 40 degrees C / 60% RH stacked-spool test, no visible ghost-image, plus release-paper interleave for spools > 30m or > 50m depending on ink-substrate combination.

7. Common Procurement Errors in Crock Spec

  1. Specifying "colorfast" without crock differentiation. "Colorfast" is a non-contractual term that covers color-change, staining, light, and crock. Specify crock separately from color-match and lightfastness.
  2. Forgetting wet crock and perspiration for accessory programs. Dry crock alone is not enough for ribbon worn on the body, used in jewelry boxes (contact with skin oils), or stored in humid conditions.
  3. Accepting plastisol without CPSIA / REACH review. Plastisol ink contains phthalates restricted under CPSIA and REACH. Confirm phthalate-free formulation for any program touching CPSIA / REACH scope.
  4. Missing release-paper interleave for long-spool programs. Stacked-spool ghost-image is the most common failure for 100m+ spools. Specify release-paper interleave or accept the failure rate.
  5. Not specifying cure-temperature and cure-time. Ink-cure is the difference between grade 4 and grade 5 crock. Under-cured ink fails crock; over-cured ink loses color-depth. Specify cure-window from the mill.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What crock grade should I specify for printed logo ribbon that ships on a white inner-pack?

For printed logo ribbon that ships on a white inner-pack (the inner-pack becomes the white-ground for crock grading), specify dry crock grade 4-5 per ISO 105-X12 / AATCC 8. Wet crock grade 3-4 minimum for ribbon that may contact water / hand-cream / perspiration. Perspiration fastness per ISO 105-E04 (pH 5.5 + pH 8.0, 4 hours x 37 degrees C) is relevant for ribbon worn as accessory (hair-bow, wrist-bow) — grade 3-4. For printed ribbon shipped in sealed poly-bag, dry crock 4-5 alone is sufficient.

Q2: How does crock fastness differ between water-based ink and plastisol ink on printed ribbon?

Water-based ink achieves dry crock 4-5 and wet crock 3-4 on polyester and cotton, with proper cure (160-180 degrees C x 90 seconds heat-cure). Plastisol ink achieves dry crock 5 and wet crock 4-5 but contains phthalates (CPSIA + REACH restricted) and limits OEKO-TEX Class I / II compliance. Dye-sublimation achieves dry crock 5 and wet crock 4-5 with no chemical-binder (polyester-only). Pigment ink achieves dry crock 4-5 and wet crock 3-4 with proper cure.

Q3: What is fabric-to-fabric crock and why does it matter for stacked ribbon spools?

Fabric-to-fabric crock is ink transfer from printed-face to unprinted-face of the adjacent ribbon layer on a wound spool. Over 30-90 days at ambient warehouse temperature (accelerated at 40 degrees C / 60% RH), the ink migrates and creates a ghost-image on the unprinted-face. Test method: wind 50m printed ribbon, store 30 days at 40 degrees C / 60% RH, inspect for ghost-image. Accept spec: no visible ghost-image at 30 days. Specify release-paper or tissue-paper interleave for spools > 30m or > 50m depending on ink-substrate.

Q4: Does perspiration fastness apply to ribbon used in gift packaging?

Perspiration fastness per ISO 105-E04 is most relevant for ribbon worn as accessory (hair-bow, wrist-bow, hat-trim, scarf-trim). For pure gift-packaging ribbon (used only as decorative wrap, not worn), perspiration fastness is generally not required. Many luxury brands specify it anyway as a brand-quality halo. If your ribbon program includes any accessory end-use (hair-bow, wrist-bow), specify ISO 105-E04 pH 5.5 + pH 8.0, grade 3-4. For pure gift-packaging ribbon, perspiration fastness can be downgraded to optional.
