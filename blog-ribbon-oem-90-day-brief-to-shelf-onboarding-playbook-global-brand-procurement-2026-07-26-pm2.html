Stage 1 — RFI and Capability Assessment (Days 1-12)

The RFI (Request for Information) and capability assessment is the macro-stage where the brand filters 30-80 candidate ribbon OEM partners down to a shortlist of 3-5. Days 1-12 cover: (a) RFI questionnaire sent to candidates covering production capacity, substrate breadth, color matching, print methods, finishing capability, OEKO-TEX / GRS / BSCI / SMETA / FSC certifications, sub-tier transparency, financial health, IT/ERP, and multi-year supply agreement readiness — typically a 28-40 question instrument; (b) desk-top review of candidate responses scored on a 100-point scale across 8 domains (capacity 20, quality 18, compliance 14, sub-tier 10, financial 8, IT 6, commercial 8, references 16); (c) on-site capability assessment by the brand's procurement team or a third-party auditor (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek) covering 38 audit points across the same 8 domains; (d) reference checks with 2-3 existing brand partners of each shortlisted OEM; (e) final shortlist of 3-5 OEM partners with documented scoring, red-flag identification, and conditional-go / no-go decisions. Deliverable: a shortlist memo signed by the brand's procurement director, attached to the 2026 sourcing strategy. KPI: 100% of shortlisted OEM partners score ≥ 80/100 on the capability assessment, 0 red-flag failures, ≥ 2 positive reference checks. Pitfall: a brand that skips the on-site audit and accepts the OEM's self-declared capability deck ends up qualifying a paper-only partner — 60-72% of the structural risks that cause program failure live below the self-declared surface.

Stage 2 — Sample Development and Lab-Dip Approval (Days 13-35)

Once the shortlist is locked, the brand buyer kicks off sample development in parallel with all 3-5 shortlisted OEM partners. Days 13-35 cover: (a) brand-side brief-of-record with 28 mandatory fields (substrate, width, color system, print method, finishing, packaging, compliance, volume, lead time, target landed cost); (b) OEM-side feasibility check on substrate, width, color gamut, print registration, compliance cert applicability, and cost; (c) artwork engineering with file format validation (AI/EPS/PDF, vector), color space conversion (PMS to substrate-specific ICC), repeat length optimization; (d) lab dip on production-equivalent substrate under D65 / A / UV light, ΔE measurement against the Pantone target; (e) pre-production sample (PPS) on production equipment with full finishing and packaging; (f) brand-buyer sign-off on the chosen lab dip and PPS, with documented ΔE ≤ 1.0 and 100% spec adherence. Deliverable: a signed PPS pack per shortlisted OEM partner, with lab-dip approval sheet, ΔE reading, and stage-gate sign-off form. KPI: PPS approval within 22 days, ΔE ≤ 1.0, ≤ 3 lab-dip rounds, ≤ 2 PPS rounds. Pitfall: a brand that uses the PPS round for spec changes loses 7-10 days per change cycle and stretches Stage 2 from 22 days to 35-45 days.

Stage 3 — Pre-Production Validation (Days 30-50)

The pre-production validation stage moves the OEM from sample-approval to production-ready. Days 30-50 cover: (a) production scheduling with line reservation and capacity booking for the brand program's annual volume; (b) full-scale production trial run on the validated spec with first-piece inspection, in-process quality checks, and pre-shipment inspection per AQL 2.5; (c) tooling and die custody documentation — brand-buyer-owned tooling held under documented custody with annual audit; (d) compliance documentation refresh — OEKO-TEX / GRS / BSCI / SMETA / FSC cert validity confirmed, REACH / CPSIA / Prop 65 / EU CBAM RSL conformance re-tested, sub-tier disclosure refreshed; (e) freight and Incoterms agreement — FOB / DDP / CIF terms with port-of-export, freight forwarder, container-loading plan; (f) payment terms agreement — typically 30% T/T deposit + 70% against B/L copy for new relationships, with a path to L/C or OA after 12-18 months. Deliverable: a production-ready file package per shortlisted OEM partner, with capacity reservation confirmation, tooling custody, compliance pack, freight terms, and payment terms. KPI: 100% of pre-production deliverables signed off within 20 days, 0 missing compliance docs, 0 capacity conflicts. Pitfall: skipping tooling custody documentation opens IP risk — a brand that does not have the dies and cylinders under documented custody can lose the spec to a competing buyer who pays for new tooling.

Stage 4 — Pilot Run and Quality Validation (Days 45-65)

The pilot run is the first production-scale run on the validated spec. Days 45-65 cover: (a) full production run on the validated spec, typically 5-15% of order quantity or 500-1,500 meters; (b) in-process quality checks at 25%, 50%, 75% completion — color consistency, print registration, width tolerance, hand-feel, defect rate; (c) pre-shipment inspection per AQL 2.5 with documented sampling plan and defect classification; (d) pilot-run shipment to the brand buyer's distribution center or nominated 3PL; (e) brand-buyer pilot acceptance within 7 days of receipt, with sign-off on quality, packaging, and documentation. Deliverable: a pilot-run shipment with 100% AQL pass, quality inspection report, defect-rate log, and brand-buyer pilot acceptance. KPI: pilot run within AQL 2.5, defect rate ≤ 0.8%, on-time pilot delivery, brand-buyer acceptance in ≤ 7 days. Pitfall: skipping the pilot run to save 14 days leads to mass-production defect rates 3-5x higher, customer-facing quality incidents, and 6-8 week rework cycles. The pilot run is the OEM's insurance policy against an unmanaged production ramp.

Stage 5 — Mass-Production Ramp (Days 60-90)

The mass-production ramp stage delivers the first full-volume shipment and validates steady-state throughput. Days 60-90 cover: (a) full-scale production run on the validated spec, with documented daily throughput target; (b) mid-run quality audits at 25%, 50%, 75% completion; (c) pre-shipment inspection per AQL 2.5; (d) packing per brand-buyer spec, freight booking per agreed Incoterms, customs documentation (COO, packing list, invoice, compliance certs); (e) on-time shipment to brand-buyer DC or nominated 3PL; (f) hand-off to ongoing replenishment cadence. Deliverable: mass-production shipment per agreed volume, AQL pass, full documentation, and replenishment cadence locked. KPI: 100% on-time mass-production delivery, AQL pass, fill rate ≥ 99%, defect rate ≤ 0.5%, replenishment cadence pre-wired with monthly or quarterly volume forecast. Pitfall: treating mass-production as the end of the OEM relationship. The 90-day playbook transitions to a replenishment cadence that maintains the validated spec, color lot continuity, and multi-year supply.

Stage 6 — Replenishment Hand-Off and Multi-Year Supply (Days 85-90)

The final stage of the 90-day playbook is the hand-off to a replenishment program. Days 85-90 cover: (a) replenishment cadence lock-in — monthly, quarterly, or just-in-time (JIT) volume forecast; (b) safety stock agreement — typically 4-8 weeks of cover for fast-moving SKUs; (c) supplier scorecard kick-off — monthly KPI tracking on delivery, quality, responsiveness, and cost; (d) multi-year supply agreement signature with documented SLA, capacity reservation, and price-volume terms; (e) quarterly business review (QBR) cadence — quarterly review of scorecard, capacity, and continuous improvement; (f) tooling and die custody annual audit. Deliverable: a multi-year supply agreement, supplier scorecard template, and QBR calendar. KPI: 100% replenishment cadence hit rate, ≥ 95% multi-year renewal rate, 0 supply disruption events. Pitfall: a brand that treats the 90-day onboarding as a one-time project loses the institutional knowledge — the replenishment hand-off is what turns a transactional OEM into a strategic partner.

The 8 Decision Gates and Their Documentation Cadence

The 90-day playbook institutionalizes 8 decision gates, each with documented sign-off. Gate 1 (Day 12): shortlist lock — brand procurement director signs the shortlist memo. Gate 2 (Day 22): lab-dip approval — brand creative director signs the chosen lab-dip ΔE sheet. Gate 3 (Day 35): PPS approval — brand merchandising signs the PPS pack. Gate 4 (Day 50): pre-production sign-off — brand quality manager signs the production-ready file package. Gate 5 (Day 65): pilot-run acceptance — brand merchandising signs the pilot-run acceptance form. Gate 6 (Day 80): mass-production quality release — brand quality manager signs the AQL pass. Gate 7 (Day 88): replenishment cadence lock-in — brand supply chain signs the replenishment forecast. Gate 8 (Day 90): multi-year supply agreement signature — both parties sign. KPI: 100% of gates signed within the day-by-day target, 0 unsigned gates, 0 retro-signed gates. Pitfall: a gate that is signed retroactively after the fact is a process failure — the gate exists to prevent downstream rework, not to document it.

The 11 KPI Targets Across the 6 Stages

The 90-day playbook tracks 11 KPIs across the 6 stages. (1) RFI response rate ≥ 95% within 7 days. (2) On-site audit completion within 12 days. (3) Shortlist scoring ≥ 80/100 across all candidates. (4) Lab-dip approval within 22 days. (5) Lab-dip ΔE ≤ 1.0 to Pantone target. (6) PPS rounds ≤ 2. (7) Pilot-run AQL pass at 2.5 General, 4.0 Critical. (8) Pilot-run defect rate ≤ 0.8%. (9) Mass-production AQL pass. (10) Mass-production defect rate ≤ 0.5%. (11) Multi-year renewal rate ≥ 95% at 12-month mark. KPI: every brand program should track the 11 KPIs in a supplier scorecard template, with monthly cadence. Pitfall: a brand that tracks only 3-4 KPIs (delivery, quality, cost) misses the structural indicators that predict renewal risk.

The 9 Most Common Onboarding Failure Modes

A disciplined 90-day playbook forces remediation when any of 9 failure modes are observed. (1) RFI self-declaration without on-site audit — paper-only partner. (2) Skipping feasibility check — substrate, width, or color unworkable. (3) Out-of-gamut Pantone — endless re-dips. (4) Raster artwork at 72dpi — re-trace delay. (5) PPS used as prototype — strict no-change gate violation. (6) Skipping pilot run — mass-production defect rate 3-5x higher. (7) No AQL standard documented — disputes at pre-shipment. (8) Late compliance documentation — customs hold. (9) No replenishment plan — stockout at the brand. Any single failure mode triggers a documented corrective action plan (CAP) with a 7-10 day response window. KPI: 100% of failure modes reach a documented CAP within the response window. Pitfall: a brand that does not enforce the failure-mode loop effectively waives its process discipline and inherits the OEM's risk.
