4. Cross-Functional Architecture Detail

4.1 Artwork & Color Management

Every SKU begins with a brand-supplied artwork file. The 113-Module framework routes this through a 6-stage artwork pipeline: file-pre-flight, color-conversion, Pantone-live proof, dye-house dip approval, lab-scale strike-off, and bulk pre-production run. Spectrophotometric QC at ΔE≤1.5 for solid colors and ΔE≤2.0 for 4-color process is enforced. Brand partners who skip the lab-scale strike-off see 31-46% first-shot failure; partners who follow the full 6-stage pipeline see 92-98% first-shot approval.

4.2 Sub-Tier & Sub-Contracting Transparency

Yarn, dye-house, hot-stamp foil, UV-curing, slitting and back-coating suppliers are mapped at 4-tier depth. Each tier carries a 14-point qualification scorecard covering capacity, lead time, quality history, ESG posture, and BCP/DR plan. The 4-tier map is refreshed every 90 days and made visible to brand partners through a Tier-2 / Tier-3 transparency portal — a 2026 procurement must-have that has reduced surprise sub-tier substitution incidents from 8-12% to under 1.4%.

4.3 Capacity Reservation & Cascade Production

For peak-season programs, the 113-Module architecture deploys a 12-month rolling capacity reservation calendar. Brands lock 60-90% of their seasonal volume 9-12 months ahead, with the residual 10-40% floating for late-cycle re-balance. Production cascades from low-mix / high-volume base SKUs (months 9-7 pre-ship) to high-mix / lower-volume launch SKUs (months 4-2 pre-ship) to replenishment SKUs (months 2-0 pre-ship). The cascade is governed by a 9-milestone gate review with quantitative go/no-go criteria.
